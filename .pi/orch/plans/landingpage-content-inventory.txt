2026</span>
[[h2]] The year the paradigm changed.
Before</span>
Now</span></div>
Before</span>
An idea</span></p>
Now</span>Still yours. Write it down.</p></div>
Before</span>
A requirements document</span></p>
Now</span>A note, in plain words.</p></div>
Before</span>
An estimation meeting</span></p>
Now</span>A quoted ceiling, in minutes.</p></div>
Before</span>
Sprint planning</span></p>
Now</span>The plan comes back by itself. You say yes.</p></div>
Before</span>
Writing the code</span></p>
Now</span>Gone. The agent types.</p></div>
Before</span>
A code review queue</span></p>
Now</span>The agent checks its own work. You accept.</p></div>
Before</span>
A manual test pass</span></p>
Now</span>A browser agent clicks through it.</p></div>
Before</span>
A release checklist</span></p>
Now</span>Accepted work gathers itself. When it ships is still your call.</p></div>
Before</span>
On-call at 3 a.m.</span></p>
Now</span>The agent is awake. You read it over coffee.</p></div>
Before</span>
A status report</span></p>
Now</span>Already written. It is the record.</p></div>
Before</span>
Ten tools, ten logins</span></p>
Now</span>One record, one set of rules.</p></div></div></div></section>
What is left for people</span>
[[h2]] Programming is theory building.
<figure class="kx-quote mt-10 max-w-4xl">
“Programming properly should be regarded as an activity by which the programmers form or achieve a certain kind of insight, a theory, of the matters at hand.”</p></blockquote>
Peter Naur · Programming as Theory Building, 1985</figcaption></figure>
People</span>
1</span></span>
[[h3]] Lead the vision
Decide what should exist, and what we will not build.</p></div>
2</span></span>
[[h3]] Set the constraints
The budget ceiling, who may touch what, and what done means.</p></div>
3</span></span>
[[h3]] Say yes
And be accountable for it.</p></div>
4</span></span>
[[h3]] Judge the evidence
Read the plan, the checks, and the receipt. Know when it is thin.</p></div>
5</span></span>
[[h3]] Hold the context
The things nobody wrote down.</p></div></div>
Agents</span>
[[h3]] Plan
Read the note and the code. Write what gets built, what done means, and a ceiling.</p></div>
[[h3]] Build
On its own branch, across every repo the change touches, with a second agent reviewing.</p></div>
[[h3]] Check
Click through the work in a real browser. Keep the screenshots. Say what could not be verified.</p></div>
[[h3]] Watch
Read the logs and alerts. Catch the issue first. Work the fix.</p></div></div>
The one rule</span>
No agent has more authority than the people around it. Nothing costs money before a person says yes. Every decision that matters comes back to a person.</p></div></div></section>

The whole company</span>
[[h2]] Many notes. Many agents. One record.
Every team writes notes and runs agents. All of it lands in one record, under the same rules.</p>
Four questions that matter</span>
[[h3]] What is moving?
Work In Progress</span>
Every initiative, who leads it, and what phase it is in.</p></div>
[[h3]] What is wrong?
Issues</span>
Incidents with a severity, an owner, and a timeline.</p></div>
[[h3]] What shipped?
Releases</span>
Accepted work, gathered and released by a person.</p></div>
[[h3]] Who carried it?
Leaderboard</span>
People by what they were part of. Models by what they return for what they cost.</p></div></div></div>
<strong class="font-medium text-ink">Activities</strong>: One timeline of the turning points: started, approved, fixed, shipped, with people’s own words next to each.</p>
The catalog</span>
[[h3]] Each product lives in a Software System.
A software catalog of its repos and components. Every session reads it, so the next note starts knowing the software.</p></div></div></section>
One set of rules</span>
[[h2]] Authority, money, evidence. The same for every person and every agent.
Enforced on the server, never by a prompt.</p>
[[h3]] Authority
Enterprise IAM is built in.</p><ul class="kx-checklist mt-2 text-sm text-secondary">
Least-privilege roles you define, and groups</li>
Access scoped to exact Systems</li>
Temporary access with a reason and an expiry</li>
Service accounts that can never hold human authority</li>
One audit trail across app, API, and MCP</li></ul></div>
[[h3]] Money
Every piece of work is quoted as a ceiling before it runs.</p><ul class="kx-checklist mt-2 text-sm text-secondary">
Your yes locks the ceiling</li>
Quotes calibrated from your own delivery history</li>
New automation runs in shadow on real work before it may decide anything</li>
Provider bill shown separately, never marked up</li></ul></div>
[[h3]] Evidence
A permanent receipt for every delivery.</p><ul class="kx-checklist mt-2 text-sm text-secondary">
Quoted, settled, provider cost, evidence</li>
The agent that builds never judges its own work</li>
Budgets stop before the line, not after</li>
Retries have limits; real decisions come back to a person</li></ul></div></div>
Four kinds of session</span>
Each one is a session on the same Software System.</p>
[[h3]] Project Management
</div>
A requirement becomes milestones, sprints, and tickets. Each ticket becomes a plan.</p></div>
[[h3]] Engineering
</div>
A plan becomes a yes, tested pull requests, and a receipt.</p></div>
[[h3]] QA
</div>
A browser agent clicks through what shipped and reports only what it ran.</p></div>
[[h3]] Ops
</div>
The agent watches logs and alerts, opens the issue, and works the fix.</p></div></div></div></div></section>
Keep your agent</span>
